Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit

Read every statistic beside its n and its decile table. The Hosmer–Lemeshow statistic scales with subgroup size: a clinically irrelevant deviation becomes significant in a large subgroup, while a large deviation can go undetected in a small one. Values are therefore not comparable across subgroups of different size, and subgroups must not be ranked by p-value. This is why Kramer & Zimmerman (2007) require the statistic to be reported together with the number of patients, the per-decile observed-versus-expected table below, and the adjunct calibration measures in the table above — never on its own.

Appendix: How to read this report

Discrimination

Discrimination measures how well the model separates positive from negative cases.

AUROC (Area Under the ROC Curve)
Probability that the model ranks a random positive case higher than a random negative case. Range [0, 1]; 0.5 = no discrimination; 1.0 = perfect separation.
AUPRC (Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve)
Summarises precision across all recall levels. More informative than AUROC for low-prevalence outcomes. Higher is better; baseline equals the prevalence.
Sensitivity (True Positive Rate)
Proportion of true positive cases correctly identified at the chosen threshold. = TP / (TP + FN).
Specificity (True Negative Rate)
Proportion of true negative cases correctly identified at the chosen threshold. = TN / (TN + FP).
PPV (Positive Predictive Value)
Proportion of positive predictions that are truly positive. = TP / (TP + FP).
NPV (Negative Predictive Value)
Proportion of negative predictions that are truly negative. = TN / (TN + FN).

Calibration

Calibration measures whether predicted probabilities match observed event rates.

Calibration intercept
Intercept from logistic recalibration with slope fixed to 1 (Steyerberg 2009). Perfect calibration = 0. Positive values indicate the model underestimates risk on average; negative values indicate overestimation.
Calibration slope
Slope from logistic recalibration with free intercept and slope. Perfect calibration = 1. Values < 1 indicate overconfident predictions (predicted probabilities too extreme); values > 1 indicate underconfident predictions.
Brier score
Mean squared difference between predicted probabilities and binary outcomes. Range [0, 1]; lower is better.
ECE (Expected Calibration Error)
Weighted average of absolute calibration error across equal-frequency bins. Lower is better.
ICI (Integrated Calibration Index)
Mean absolute difference between LOESS-smoothed observed probabilities and predicted probabilities (Austin & Steyerberg 2019). Lower is better; more robust than ECE as it avoids binning artefacts.
Hosmer–Lemeshow statistic
Chi-square-distributed goodness-of-fit statistic comparing observed with expected events across deciles of predicted risk. A large value indicates lack of fit. It scales with sample size, so it is not comparable between subgroups of different size and must be read alongside n, the per-decile table, and the calibration measures above (Kramer & Zimmerman 2007).
Hosmer–Lemeshow degrees of freedom
Equal to the number of risk groups used. The classical convention subtracts 2, which is the penalty for estimating an intercept and slope on the data under test; this package evaluates predictions supplied by a model fitted elsewhere, so no degrees of freedom are spent and none are subtracted.
Decile slope and intercept
Least-squares regression of the observed on the expected event rate across risk groups, recommended by Kramer & Zimmerman (2007) as an adjunct to the statistic. Perfect calibration gives a slope of 1 and an intercept of 0.

Clinical utility (decision curves)

Decision curve analysis quantifies the clinical value of using the model at a given decision threshold.

Net benefit
TP/n − FP/n × pt/(1 − pt), where pt is the decision threshold. Higher is better. A model is clinically useful when its net benefit exceeds both 0 (treat none) and the treat-all reference.
Standardised net benefit
Net benefit divided by prevalence (Naderalvojoud 2025). Normalised to a [0, 1] scale for comparison across cohorts with different prevalences.
Treat-all reference
Net benefit if all patients receive treatment. = prevalence − (1 − prevalence) × pt/(1 − pt). Becomes strongly negative at high thresholds.
Treat-none reference
Net benefit = 0. The baseline of no intervention.

Gap statistics

Gap statistics quantify the largest disparity between subgroups for each metric. All gaps are non-negative; 0 = perfect parity.

Equal opportunity difference
max − min of sensitivity across subgroups. Measures fairness in true positive identification.
Predictive equality
max − min of false positive rate (1 − specificity) across subgroups. Measures fairness in false alarm rates.
Equalized odds gap
Maximum of equal opportunity difference and predictive equality.
Statistical parity
max − min of positive prediction rate across subgroups. Measures whether the model predicts positive at equal rates.
Max intercept / slope gap
Maximum pairwise difference in calibration intercept or slope across subgroups, with bootstrap confidence intervals.
Max net benefit gap
Maximum across all thresholds of the maximum pairwise net-benefit difference between subgroups, with bootstrap confidence intervals.
